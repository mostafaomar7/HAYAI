# What was configured in the CMS, 2026-10-06

None of this is code — it is content and configuration in the dashboard,
recorded here because it is invisible in a diff and somebody will ask later
why the navigation exists.

## The site had no navigation at all

`menus.header` and `menus.footer` were both empty, so the header carried a
logo and a language switch and nothing else. Every page on the site was
reachable only by typing its URL: orphaned for a visitor, and orphaned for a
crawler, which reads internal links as what the site considers important.

| Menu | Items |
|---|---|
| header | How it works · Insurance · Emergency & ICU · Doctors |
| footer | About · How it works · Insurance · Emergency & ICU · Doctors · FAQ |

Four in the header on purpose. A header that lists everything makes the one
thing a patient came for harder to find, and the footer is where the rest
belongs.

Both locales are set. Arabic items resolve to the Arabic slugs
(`/ar/كيف-تعمل-حياة`), not to transliterations.

## The conversions a patient could make: none

Before this there was no patient-facing anything. Every form on the site was
B2B — contact, partnership, demo, quote — and the only `tel:` link in the
whole site was `123`, the national ambulance number. A patient who arrived
from an ad had no way to leave a trace, which is the same as an ad budget
with no measurable result.

### Form: `patient-waitlist` (id 7)

Two fields, name and mobile number, plus consent. Nothing else — every extra
field costs completions, and nothing else is needed.

**Framed as early access, not as a medical request, and that is load-bearing.**
A form that asks about symptoms or a condition makes the page `sensitive`,
and on a sensitive page the advertising tags do not fire at all: the
conversion would happen and Google would never hear about it. The
non-medical framing is what keeps the conversion visible.

The mobile number is also the join key. When the patient app ships, somebody
who signed up as `010…` and later books in the app with the same number can
be matched — the cheap version of linking an app booking back to the ad that
produced it, collecting its data from today rather than from launch day.

### CTAs

| key | type | where |
|---|---|---|
| `whatsapp-hayai` | whatsapp | sticky mobile, sitewide |
| `call-hayai` | call | footer |
| `get-app-patients` | external_link | hero |
| `get-app-partners` | external_link | bottom, About |
| `patient-waitlist` | custom_form | inline, on the pages ads land on |

The number `+20 103 657 0577` was already live on the parked coming-soon page
and is answered by Islam; it is now the site's number as well, and is in the
`MedicalOrganization` node where Google can read it.

The WhatsApp link carries a prefilled message naming the page, so whoever
answers knows where the patient came from without any tracking at all.

**Both store links are tagged**, so Play Console reports installs per
campaign with no work in the app:

```
?id=com.hayai.app&referrer=utm_source%3Dwebsite%26utm_medium%3Dreferral%26utm_campaign%3Dpatient-app
```

Neither app resolved on Play when this was written (`com.whatsapp` as a
control returned 200, both HAYAI packages 404), so the links are correct and
will start working when review finishes. Worth re-checking before any
campaign spends money.

## Settings

The Arabic `organization.description` was serving the English sentence, on
the Arabic footer and inside the Arabic `MedicalOrganization` node. Replaced
with Arabic a patient would actually read, not a translation of the English.

`phone`, `whatsapp` and `email` were all null, so the schema node named an
organisation with no way to reach it.

## Verified

- Navigation renders in both locales, header and footer, desktop and mobile.
- A submission carrying `gclid` is stored on the lead with its campaign:
  `LD-261006-GWXSG9`, `gclid=TestGclid_ABC123xyz`. **This is the first proof
  of the whole chain — ad click, server capture, browser, API, database.**
  That lead is test data and should be deleted before launch.
- `scripts/seo-gate.mjs`: all required checks pass.
- `scripts/check-tracking.sh`: passes.
- No horizontal overflow at 390px (`scrollWidth === clientWidth === 390`),
  measured under real mobile emulation. An earlier screenshot suggested
  otherwise; headless Chrome ignores the viewport meta without emulation and
  clips the desktop layout, which looks identical to an overflow.
